### Task 1

1. Read the data and select participants from the US. Identify theoretically plausible psychometric models for the Hyper Sensitive Narcissism Scale (HSNS) and Dirty Dozen (DDM). Fit and compare alternative factor-analytic models for each scale.

### Task 2

1. Select the psychometric model for each scale that provides the best balance of model fit, interpretability, identification, parsimony, and proper and trustworthy parameter (or absence of anomalous parameter) estimates. Clearly justify the model-selection decision and distinguish statistical from substantive arguments.

### Task 3

1. Investigate the degree of measurement invariance across gender for both selected measurement models using appropriate statistical methods.

### Task 4

Based on the invariance results, determine which comparisons across gender are psychometrically defensible and meaningful —for example, comparisons of factor structure, associations, variances, or latent means—and which comparisons should not be made.

### Task 5

Examine the relationships between the latent dimensions of the HSNS and DDM while accounting for measurement error. Avoid inappropriate analyses such as treating estimated factor scores as error-free when a joint latent-variable model is feasible. Test whether these latent relationships differ across gender.

### Task 6

Test whether the selected measurement models and the main substantive findings replicate across the remaining countries. Distinguish exact replication, approximate replication, and substantive replication.

### Task 7

Diagnose sources of model misfit and non-invariance at the item level. Identify problematic items using appropriate diagnostics (e.g., residuals, modification indices, DIF parameters, cross-loadings). Determine whether problems reflect local dependence, cross-loadings, differential item functioning, or poor item quality. Introduce modifications only when they are substantively defensible and verify that conclusions are not driven by purely data-driven model tinkering.

### Task 8

Evaluate psychometric scoring and reliability. Determine whether total scores, subscale scores, latent factor scores, or a general-factor score are justified for each instrument. Estimate suitable reliability measures and investigate whether reliability and measurement precision differ across traits, gender, or countries.

### Task 9

Conduct a robustness and sensitivity analysis. Examine whether the main psychometric conclusions depend on reasonable analytical choices, such as treating items as ordinal versus continuous, estimator choice, missing-data treatment, inclusion of cross-loadings, correlated residuals, or alternative plausible factor structures. Report which conclusions are robust and which are specification-dependent.

### Task 10

Perform out-of-sample psychometric validation. Develop the measurement model using a predefined training subset (e.g., US or a random training sample) and evaluate the resulting model in held-out participants or countries. Assess whether model fit, parameter estimates, factor correlations, invariance conclusions, and substantive interpretations generalize without further model modification.
